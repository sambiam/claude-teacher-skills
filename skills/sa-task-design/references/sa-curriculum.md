## The frame

Every year level is written as **UNDERSTAND / DO / BE**:

| Ring | Name | Content |
|---|---|---|
| UNDERSTAND | Knowledge | Number, Algebra, Measurement, Space, Statistics, Probability — each with *What it is*, *Why it matters*, conceptual understandings, and supporting content descriptions carrying AC9M codes |
| DO | Capabilities | Problem solving · Developing understanding · Fluency and flexibility · Reasoning |
| BE | Dispositions | Resilient · Resourceful · Reflective |

A task built by this skill must name **which disposition and which capability it
develops**, not just which content descriptor it covers. That is the point of the
SA framing and it is the thing most task planning leaves out.

## Dispositions (years 7-10 wording, verbatim)

### Resilient
*This disposition may be evident when learners, for example:*
- communicate the worth of mathematics and the important and relevant role it plays in their lives
- approach mathematics with a positive attitude and a willingness to explore and engage
- embrace struggle and mistakes as natural and expected parts of learning, take risks to try new strategies and communicate their thinking even if uncertain, developing their belief in themselves as capable learners of mathematics
- persevere when working mathematically, show determination and a willingness to overcome challenges and experience the rewards of 'figuring it out'.

### Resourceful
*This disposition may be evident when learners, for example:*
- show inquisitiveness by seeking information, working through a process of planning, and applying strategies and heuristics to find solutions
- draw on their knowledge, skills, procedures and processes to seek connections and make meaning
- engage with others as collaborative partners in learning, acknowledge their contribution, challenge views and ideas, remain open to having their own thinking and approaches challenged, and actively contribute to group outcomes
- purposefully choose and use physical and digital tools, strategies, representations and heuristics.

### Reflective
*This disposition may be evident when learners, for example:*
- reflect on themselves as learners, including strengths, challenges and engagement when thinking and working mathematically
- reflect on what they know about the task and strategies that could be used to plan and manage learning
- plan and monitor approaches, evaluate effectiveness, and seek and apply feedback to adapt mathematical approaches and outcomes.

**Design consequence.** Resilience needs a task with a *stuck point* that is
survivable. Resourcefulness needs a task where **more than one strategy or
representation is viable** and the student picks. Reflectiveness needs a task with
a **built-in moment of looking back** — a comparison of approaches, a "was that
reasonable", a "what would you do differently". If your task has none of these
three, it is an exercise, not an SA task.

## Capabilities (years 7-10 wording, verbatim)

### Problem solving
- applying a problem-solving process, such as:
  - identifying and interpreting information in routine and non-routine problems and designing investigations
  - planning, formulating and choosing strategies and representations
  - applying new or known strategies and heuristics such as working backwards, simplifying the problem and using equations, and exploring alternatives
  - verifying and reflecting on the reasonableness of approaches and solutions
- thinking and working independently and collaboratively, communicating solutions, discussing ideas and wonderings, listening to and building on others' thinking, comparing varied approaches, engaging in debates and giving and receiving feedback by sharpening each other's arguments through constructive critiques.

### Developing understanding
- building on prior knowledge by connecting new ideas with what they already know and sharing commonalities and differences between content and concepts
- interpreting mathematical information, describing thinking mathematically and representing concepts in different ways.

### Fluency and flexibility
- applying definitions, facts and theorems, and manipulating objects, expressions, relations and equations to find solutions and connect their conceptual understanding to learned strategies and procedures
- choosing and using appropriate representations, processes and approximations, recognising robust ways of answering questions.

### Reasoning
- communicating their mathematical thinking and reasoning, reflecting on and explaining their choices, justifying strategies used and convincing others
- making conjectures from generalisations of patterns or relationships and evaluating conjectures through deductive reasoning and developing mathematical proofs
- adapting the known to the unknown, comparing and contrasting related ideas, and transferring learning from one context to another.

## Year 7

**Learning standard (extract).** Learners *demonstrate perseverance and
self-reliance when thinking and working independently and collaboratively to
engage with routine and non-routine problems* … *develop a productive disposition
to mathematics through experiences which prompt curiosity. They reflect on and
explain their thinking and embrace their mistakes as part of productive struggle.*

### Conceptual understandings
- **Number** — Rational numbers can be represented in infinitely many different but equivalent ways. Where a number has repeated factors, it can be expressed using exponential notation. Rational numbers can be approximated to support judgements of magnitude and reasonableness. The inverse relationships between + / − and × / ÷ can be used to operate on rational numbers (including integers). Multiplying a positive number by a number between 0 and 1 results in a smaller number; dividing by such a number results in a larger number. Fractions, percents, ratios and rates are different ways of representing a proportional relationship.
- **Algebra** — Patterns and relationships between variables can be described algebraically and graphed to solve problems and support inferences. Algebraic expressions can be represented in infinitely many equivalent ways. Commutative and associative properties apply to + and × but not − and ÷. The distributive property supports efficient calculation.
- **Measurement** — Parallel or intersecting lines have properties used to deduce angle relationships. Measurable attributes of 2D shapes and 3D objects can be determined from known properties. In any circle the ratio of circumference to diameter is always π.
- **Space** — Objects can be represented in 2D in different ways. Shapes can be classified and compared by geometrical properties. Transformations of points and 2D shapes can be represented on a Cartesian plane.
- **Statistics** — Data can be organised and represented in different ways to inform, justify and persuade. Variation in data can be measured to describe and compare data sets.
- **Probability** — Outcomes of events can be described and their likelihood measured. Theoretical probabilities can predict relative frequencies of experimental data.

### Content descriptors

`N03` expanded notation with powers of 10 · `N04` equivalent rationals on a number
line · `N05` rounding and estimation to check reasonableness · `N06` four
operations with positive rationals · `N07` addition/subtraction of integers ·
`N08` ratios · `N09` modelling with rationals and percentages incl. financial.
`AC9M7A01` variables in everyday formulas · `A02` forming expressions ·
`A03` one-variable linear equations, natural-number solutions · `A04` relationships
in graphs from authentic data · `A05` tables of values from visually growing
patterns; plotting · `A06` manipulating multi-variable formulas with digital tools.
`AC9M7M01` area of triangles and parallelograms · `M02` volume of right prisms ·
`M03` π and features of circles · `M04` angles on parallel lines and a transversal ·
`M05` angle sum of triangle, extended to other shapes · `M06` modelling with ratios.
`AC9M7SP01` 2D representations of objects · `SP02` classifying triangles,
quadrilaterals, polygons · `SP03` transformations on the Cartesian plane ·
`SP04` sorting/classifying algorithms.
`AC9M7ST01` range, median, mean, mode and which is useful · `ST02` displays incl.

chance experiments and simulations.

## Year 8

**Learning standard (extract).** *…perseverance and self-reliance … routine and
non-routine problems … make informed choices when using rational and irrational
numbers … model and investigate linear relations in various contexts, applying
analytical skills to develop conjectures and generalised patterns.*

### Conceptual understandings
- **Number** — Rational and irrational numbers can be expressed in multiple forms depending on context and purpose. Exponential laws describe the patterns in operations involving exponential notation.
- **Algebra** — Equivalent algebraic expressions and solutions to equations can be determined by applying properties of operations and numbers. Linear relations between two variables can be represented algebraically and graphically, leading to conjectures and generalised patterns.
- **Measurement** — Attributes (length, area, volume, rates, angles) of 1-, 2- and 3-dimensional objects including irregular and composite shapes can be quantified with appropriate units. Duration can be quantified including 12- and 24-hour time across multiple time zones.
- **Space** — Congruency and similarity can be established and used for triangles and other shapes. Comparison of sides and angles between two triangles establishes congruence or similarity from which unknown measures can be deduced. Position and location in 3D can be represented using a coordinate system.
- **Statistics** — To make valid inferences about populations, conclusions must be drawn from representative samples. A representative sample depends on the population and the question.
- **Probability** — The sample space for 2 events can be presented diagrammatically in a variety of ways. Complementary events are mutually exclusive and together cover all outcomes.

### Content descriptors
`AC9M8N01` irrational numbers incl. √ and π · `N02` exponent laws, positive
integer and zero exponents · `N03` terminating and recurring decimals ·
`N04` four operations with integers and rationals · `N05` modelling with rationals
and percentages incl. financial.
`AC9M8A01` create/expand/factorise/rearrange/simplify linear expressions ·
`A02` graph linear relations; solve linear equations and one-variable inequalities ·
`A03` modelling with linear relations · `A04` experimenting with linear functions,
conjecturing and generalising.
`AC9M8M01` area and perimeter of irregular and composite shapes ·
`M02` volume/capacity of right prisms · `M03` circumference and area of a circle ·
`M04` duration across time zones · `M05` rates · `M06` **Pythagoras' theorem** ·
`M07` modelling with ratios and rates.
`AC9M8SP01` conditions for congruence and similarity · `SP02` properties of
quadrilaterals via congruent triangles · `SP03` 3D coordinates · `SP04` congruence
/ similarity algorithms.
`AC9M8ST01` census, sampling, experiment, observation · `ST02` random and
non-random sampling · `ST03` variation between samples, effect of sample size ·
`ST04` statistical investigations with samples.
`AC9M8P01` complementary events sum to 1 · `P02` two-way tables, tree diagrams,
Venn diagrams for 2 events · `P03` repeated experiments and simulations for
compound events.

## Year 9

**Learning standard (extract).** *…appreciate the value and logic of mathematics
as they engage with rich questions, use multiple strategies and representations …
demonstrate perseverance and adaptability while embracing mistakes…*

### Conceptual understandings
- **Number** — Rational and irrational numbers can be represented in many forms, including on a number line, and used to find exact and approximate values.
- **Algebra** — The parameters of quadratics and other functions determine the features of the related graph and can be varied depending on purpose, context and application. Expressions with integer and variable bases can be simplified using the exponent laws. On a Cartesian plane, the coordinates of 2 distinct points determine a range of measures of their orientation.
- **Measurement** — Spatial relationships in 1, 2 and 3 dimensions can be quantified using appropriate units. Calculations are affected by sources of error and interpreted in context.
- **Space** — Trigonometric ratios are an application of the property of similarity for right-angled triangles.
- **Statistics** — Sampling methods can affect and bias results. Different representations can be used to compare centre, shape and spread. Statistical reports support verifiable conclusions, acknowledging the strength of the evidence.
- **Probability** — Theoretical probabilities are based on the characteristics of an experiment. The likelihood of an event can be affected by another event that has already taken place. Compound-event probability relates to the probabilities of the associated simple events.

### Content descriptors
`AC9M9N01` real number system; problems with real numbers using digital tools.
`AC9M9A01` exponent laws with integer exponents, extended to variables ·
`A02` simplify, expand binomial products, factorise monic quadratics ·
`A03` gradient, midpoint and distance between two points · `A04` graph quadratics,
solve quadratic equations · `A05` modelling with linear or quadratic functions ·
`A06` effect of varying parameters on graphs.
`AC9M9M01` volume and surface area of right prisms and cylinders ·
`M02` very small/large measurements, scientific notation · `M03` spatial problems
with angle properties, scale, similarity, Pythagoras and trigonometry ·
`M04` absolute, relative and percentage error · `M05` modelling with direct
proportion, rates, ratio and scale.
`AC9M9SP01` constancy of sine, cosine, tangent via similarity · `SP02` enlargement
transformation; what stays the same · `SP03` geometric construction algorithms.
`AC9M9ST01` analysing reported surveys · `ST02` how sampling and representation
choices support a point of view · `ST03` comparative representations, centre,
spread, shape, outliers · `ST04` choosing displays and justifying ·
`ST05` statistical investigations incl. economic and financial data.
`AC9M9P01` outcomes for compound events with and without replacement ·
`P02` relative frequencies; "and", inclusive "or", exclusive "or" ·
`P03` repeated experiments and simulations comparing simple and compound events.

## Year 10

**Learning standard (extract).** *…engage with rich questions, use multiple
strategies and representations … perseverance and adaptability while embracing
mistakes … construct diagrams that represent relationships between entities,
determine the optimum pathway, justify their choice and test in real situations.*

### Conceptual understandings
- **Number** — The results of single and multiple calculations are affected by using approximation as opposed to exact values.
- **Algebra** — Relationships between variables can be described and modelled by linear, quadratic and exponential functions applied in many contexts.
- **Measurement** — Attributes of 1-, 2- and 3-dimensional objects are described and measured in appropriate standardised units. Measurement errors can impact the accuracy of results.
- **Space** — Situations involving angles, distance and direction can be quantified using geometric properties and theorems.
- **Statistics** — Data can be represented in many ways to establish relationships, considering accuracy and bias. Claims and inference based on data should acknowledge ethical considerations and potential sources of bias.
- **Probability** — Conditional probability can be described, determined and interpreted for multiple events and applied in real-life contexts.

### Content descriptors
`AC9M10N01` effect of approximation in repeated calculations vs exact
representations.
`AC9M10A01` expand, factorise, simplify; exponent laws with variables ·
`A02` linear inequalities and simultaneous linear equations in 2 variables ·
`A03` exponential relations, algebraic ↔ graphical · `A04` modelling growth and
decay incl. financial gain and loss · `A05` experimenting with functions,
conjecturing and generalising.
`AC9M10M01` surface area and volume of composite objects · `M02` logarithmic
scales in applied contexts · `M03` Pythagoras and trigonometry incl. elevation
and depression · `M04` impact of measurement error incl. economic and financial ·
`M05` modelling with proportion and scaling.
`AC9M10SP01` deductive reasoning and proofs with plane shapes ·
`SP02` **networks and network diagrams**, connectedness · `SP03` algorithms and
digital tools for spatial problems.
`AC9M10ST01` analysing claims and inference in media reports incl. ethics and

statistical investigations and limitations of inference.
`AC9M10P01` conditional probability language: 'if…then', 'given', 'of', 'knowing
that' · `P02` simulations modelling conditional probability.

## Notes for the task designer

1. **The 10 Methods / 11 Methods classes sit outside this document.** It stops at
   Year 10. For senior classes use the SACE Mathematical Methods subject outline
   and treat the SA dispositions as still applying — the BE ring is a whole-school
   commitment, not a R-10 one.
2. **`^` and `*` markers.** In the source, `*` = content description adapted for
   SA Curriculum, `^` = created for SA Curriculum. Most `^` entries are consumer
   and financial literacy, concentrated in primary years. Year 7-10 financial
   content sits inside the modelling descriptors.
3. **Modelling descriptors are where tasks live.** `N09`/`A03`/`M06`/`M07`/`A05`/
   `M05` all say *formulate the problem, choose the representation, interpret and
   communicate the solution, review the appropriateness of the model*. That
   sentence is a task specification. If a lesson only ever practises the
   procedure, those descriptors are not being taught at all.
