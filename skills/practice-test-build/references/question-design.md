# Question design

> **On a practice test**, rules 1, 4 and 5 below shift. The paper deliberately
> does *not* match the real test's question types — see the format table in
> SKILL.md — while it does match the skills, weighting and arithmetic
> difficulty. Rules 5 and 6 (no reused questions or contexts) still bind, and
> so does everything under Validity checks. The difficulty-balance section
> below is superseded by the 60/40 routine-to-complex split.

## Core requirements

1. Match the style, breadth, structure, and approximate difficulty of the
   prior assessments in the folder.
2. Apply the specified topic inclusions and exclusions exactly. A question on
   an excluded topic invalidates the paper.
3. Keep the topic balance of the prior paper unless a change was requested.
4. Match the exemplar's difficulty by default. A practice paper that runs
   harder than the real one misinforms students about where they stand, and a
   topic test that does it distorts the grade. Go harder only when the teacher
   asks, and then only where it stays fair and aligned to what was taught.
5. Use new numbers, new contexts, and new question structures. A question
   reused with the numbers changed is not a new question, however different
   the arithmetic looks.
6. Avoid repeating distinctive worded-problem contexts from the exemplars —
   these are the most recognisable part of a paper and the easiest to spot as
   recycled.
7. Keep command words simple where the task is simple: Simplify, Solve,
   Evaluate, Make [variable] the subject, Complete the table, Draw a tree
   diagram, Draw a box plot.
8. Don't over-contextualise. A paper where every question is a story problem
   tests reading as much as mathematics. Mix direct skill questions with
   applied ones.
9. Use authentic, age-appropriate contexts. Avoid artificial, unrealistic, or
   ambiguous scenarios — ambiguity in a test is unfair in a way it isn't in a
   worksheet, because the student can't ask.
10. Every question must be answerable using taught skills, with the materials
    the students are actually allowed. Check this against the brief: a
    question with untidy trigonometric values is fine with a calculator and
    unfair without one; a statistics question that assumes summary statistics
    off the device is fine with a CAS and a different question by hand. If
    there's a non-calculator section, its questions need numbers that work
    out.
11. Marks must match the expected working and complexity.
12. No hidden assumptions. If a question needs a value, give it.
13. Numerical answers should be clean unless approximation is the point.
    State rounding instructions explicitly.
14. Don't group unrelated skills under one question number. If a subpart isn't
    meaningfully connected to the ones before it, make it a separate question.
15. Avoid sequences that are repetitive or too obviously patterned — students
    start answering the pattern rather than the mathematics.

## Difficulty balance

Across the paper, aim for the same spread the prior assessments used. Typical
shape:

- **Routine fluency** — single-skill, one or two steps. Every student should
  score here.
- **Multi-step procedural** — the bulk of the paper.
- **Interpretation** — read a graph, table, or diagram and say what it means.
- **Application / worded** — modelling and context.
- **Higher-order / unfamiliar** — a small number, at the end of a topic's
  questions rather than the end of the paper, so students who run out of time
  don't lose access to a whole topic.

Check the entry point specifically: the first question of the paper, and the
first question of each topic, should be answerable by the weakest student in
the class. A paper that opens hard tells you nothing about the students it
defeats.

## Where application questions belong

**Usually include** application or interpretation questions for:
statistics, probability, linear modelling, quadratic modelling, financial
mathematics, measurement, networks, trigonometry, growth and decay,
simultaneous equations worded problems.

**Sometimes** for: functions, algebraic manipulation, graph interpretation,
equations and inequalities, sequences.

**Usually keep routine**, where the point is technical skill: simplifying
expressions, solving basic equations, factorising, expanding, substitution,
formula rearranging, basic graph features.

## Validity checks

Before the paper is done, confirm:

- Every question has a correct answer, verified independently of the question
  — worked separately, and computed rather than reasoned about
- No impossible data sets, inconsistent conditions, or invalid equations
- No question accidentally gives away the answer to another
- No question requires a skill from an excluded topic to complete
- No question requires materials the students won't have
- Diagrams contain every value the question refers to
